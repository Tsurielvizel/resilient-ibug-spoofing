Resilient I-Bug Navigation under Intermittent Signal Spoofing

Tsuriel Vizel  •  Avraham Guez
Supervisor: Dr. Andrey Zhitnikov  •  Technion – Israel Institute of Technology

The Paper in One Sentence: Imagine a simple blindfolded robot trying to reach a radio beacon inside an unknown obstacle course using only a touch bumper and a "Hot-or-Cold" signal-strength meter. We show how an intermittent "spoofing" transmitter can trick the robot into walking in circles forever—and how the robot can use the physics of its own top speed to catch the fake signal and reach the goal without adding a single new sensor!

Accessible Scientific Abstract: Minimalist autonomous robots operating in GPS-denied environments rely on local sensor feedback rather than global coordinates or odometry (wheel-rotation distance tracking). The I-Bug algorithm achieves guaranteed target convergence using only a contact sensor and a scalar signal-intensity sensor (which reports a single number—strength—without direction). However, when the receiver measures the additive sum of multiple emitters, an intermittent spoofing transmitter warps the direction of steepest signal increase (the gradient) in free space and injects false signal peaks along obstacle boundaries, trapping standard I-Bug agents in perpetual loops. We present a kinematics-based temporal anomaly filter and two counter-strategies: a Cautious strategy that preserves the exact active path of benign I-Bug ($100.00\%$ success across $1,000,000$ mazes), and a fast open-loop Inertial heuristic ($99.61\%$ success while reducing mean steps by $20.5\%$, compared to $54.68\%$ for the unprotected Naive baseline).

Reader's Guide: Quick Mathematical Notation & Terminology Key

  • Sets and Spaces ($\in, \subset, \mathbb{R}^2$): $\mathbb{R}^2$ is a flat 2D plane $(x,y)$. The bounded workspace is $\mathcal{W} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$, the obstacles are $\mathcal{O} = \{O_1, \dots, O_M\}$, and the collision-free floor is $\mathcal{F} \subset \mathcal{W}$. Writing $p \in \mathcal{F}$ reads: "position $p$ is inside the free space $\mathcal{F}$."
  • Euclidean Distance ($\|p - p_t\|_2$): Straight-line Pythagorean distance between the robot $p$ and target beacon $p_t$.
  • Scalar vs. Vector: A scalar is a single number (e.g., intensity $0.5$). A vector is an arrow with magnitude and direction. The robot's sensor measures only a scalar!
  • Gradient ($\nabla_p S$) and Partial Derivatives ($\partial_x S, \partial_y S$): $\nabla_p S$ points in the direction where signal $S$ rises fastest ("steepest uphill"). $\partial_x S$ and $\partial_y S$ measure how much $S$ changes over a tiny step along $x$ and $y$.
  • Logical & Rounding Symbols ($\iff, \lor, \lceil \cdot \rceil$): $\iff$ means "if and only if"; $\lor$ means logical "OR"; and $\lceil x \rceil$ rounds $x$ up to the next integer.

1. Introduction & Threat Model: Navigating by "Hot-and-Cold" and How Spoofing Breaks It

Under benign conditions, the target signal decays with distance as $m(p - p_t) = \|p - p_t\|_2^{-1} = \frac{1}{\|p - p_t\|_2}$, and the mission succeeds when $\|p - p_t\|_2 < R_{\text{reach}}$. At each step $k$, the robot uses three motion primitives:

  1. $u_{\text{ori}}$ (Orient to Gradient): Probes signal intensity at four nearby points to find the steepest uphill angle: $$\theta_{\nabla}(p, t) = \operatorname{atan2}\!\bigl(\partial_y S(p, t),\, \partial_x S(p, t)\bigr) \pmod{2\pi}.$$
  2. $u_{\text{fwd}}$ (Move Forward in MoveToTower): Steps straight ahead along $\theta_k$. Upon hitting a wall, it records the hit threshold $i_H \leftarrow \max(S(p_k, t_k), i_H)$, turns right by $\pi/2$, and enters FollowObstacle.
  3. $u_{\text{fol}}$ (Follow Boundary in FollowObstacle): Traces the obstacle perimeter keeping the wall on its left while tracking the local maximum $i_{\max}$. Once it climbs above $i_H$ ($i_{\max} > i_H + \epsilon$) and then detects a drop ($S(p_k, t_k) < i_{\max} - \epsilon$), it has reached the closest boundary point to the beacon—updating $i_H \leftarrow i_{\max}$ and returning to MoveToTower.
Assumption 1 (Additive RSS Superposition Field): A spoofing transmitter at $p_e \in \mathcal{F}$ switches on/off via $u(t) \in \{0, 1\}$. The sensor observes the sum of both emitters: $$S(p, t) = m(p - p_t) + e(p, t), \qquad e(p, t) = u(t)\,\frac{\alpha_e}{\|p - p_e\|_2}.$$

When $u(t)=1$, the attacker warps the spatial gradient:

$$\nabla_p S(p, t) = -\frac{p - p_t}{\|p - p_t\|_2^3} - u(t)\,\alpha_e \frac{p - p_e}{\|p - p_e\|_2^3},$$

causing: (1) Free-Space Gradient Deflection (pulling the robot toward the vector sum of both towers), and (2) Threshold Poisoning ($i_H$) (recording an artificially inflated hit intensity on a wall that can never be exceeded once $u(t) \to 0$).

2. Physics-Informed Anomaly Detection and Counter-Strategies

Assumption 2 (Kinematic Lipschitz Bound vs. Temporal Discontinuity): Over a fast sampling window $\delta t \ll \Delta t$ (maximum displacement $\delta s = v_{\max}\delta t$), the largest natural intensity change from reference $I_{\text{ref}} = 1/d$ caused by robot movement is bounded by: $$\Delta I_{\max}(I_{\text{ref}}) = \frac{\delta s}{d(d - \delta s)} = \frac{\delta s\, I_{\text{ref}}^2}{1 - \delta s\, I_{\text{ref}}}.$$ Conversely, transmitter switching ($0 \leftrightarrow 1$) creates an instantaneous step jump $e_{\min} \ge \frac{\alpha_e}{\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{W})}$ strictly larger than $\eta_{\text{spike}}\Delta I_{\max}$.

At the start of step $k$, the filter compares $I_k^- = S(p_{k-1}, t_k)$ against $I_{k-1}^+ = S(p_{k-1}, t_{k-1})$ ($\Delta I_k = I_k^- - I_{k-1}^+$) to flag activation spikes ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{spike}}$) and deactivation drops ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{drop}}$):

$$\mathcal{A}_{\text{spike}} \iff \Delta I_k > \eta_{\text{spike}}\,\Delta I_{\max}(I_{k-1}^+), \qquad \mathcal{A}_{\text{drop}} \iff (-\Delta I_k > 0.15 I_{k-1}^+) \;\lor\; (-\Delta I_k > \eta_{\text{drop}}\Delta I_{\max}(I_k^-)).$$

3. Formal Convergence Proof & The Resonance Trap

Theorem 1 (Conditional Trajectory & Convergence Preservation): Under Assumptions 1–2 (with $\eta_{\text{drop}} \le \eta_{\text{spike}}$ and $T_{\text{off}} > N_{\text{confirm}}$): (i) after removing paused steps, the Cautious robot's trajectory $\gamma_{\text{cautious}}$ is identical step-by-step to the benign I-Bug trajectory $\gamma_{\text{ideal}}$; and (ii) whenever the benign robot reaches the target in $N_{\text{ideal}} < \infty$ steps, the Cautious robot reaches the target in at most: $$N_{\text{cautious}} \le N_{\text{ideal}} + \left\lceil \frac{N_{\text{ideal}}}{T_{\text{off}} - N_{\text{confirm}}} \right\rceil (T_{\text{on}} + N_{\text{confirm}}).$$
Proposition 1 (Topological Resonance Trap in the Inertial Strategy): If the Inertial robot orbits an obstacle whose perimeter traversal time satisfies $T_{\text{orbit}} = m \cdot T_{\text{cycle}}$ and the $T_{\text{on}}$-step blind arc covers the departure arc $\mathcal{A}_L$, the robot will pass over the exit while peak detection is suspended on every revolution—trapping it in an infinite limit cycle!
